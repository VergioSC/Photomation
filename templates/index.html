How it decides

Sharpness is measured as the variance of the Laplacian.

The Laplacian is an edge filter: it reacts strongly wherever brightness changes quickly. A sharp photo is full of those fast changes, so its edge responses are spread widely and the variance is high. A blurry photo is smooth, its responses are bunched near zero, and the variance is low. One number per photo, and bigger means sharper.

1:1

Megapixels don't skew it

Every photo is scaled to 1000px on its long edge before scoring, so a 48MP file and a 12MP file of the same scene get comparable numbers — and one threshold works across your whole library.

●

Nothing is ever destroyed

Originals are only read and copied. Nothing in your input folder is moved, renamed or deleted — so a badly chosen threshold costs you nothing but a re-run.

▤

Tune it live

Scoring happens once. The threshold slider then re-sorts the results instantly, so you can find the cut-off that matches your own eye instead of trusting a default.

Three steps

Add photos

Drop them into the input/ folder. JPEG, PNG, HEIC from an iPhone, or camera RAW.

Scan

Every photo is scored once. Results stream in as they're processed, so you see progress immediately.

Drag and apply

Move the threshold until the split looks right, then write the copies into keep and reject folders.

One honest caveat

This measures edge detail across the whole frame, not whether your subject is in focus. A portrait with heavy background blur, or a minimalist shot with a lot of empty sky, will score low while being perfectly sharp. Check the reject column before you trust it, and expect to tune the threshold per shoot.
